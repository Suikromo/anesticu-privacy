# VT-001 — What Actually Happens When You Are Put Under Anesthesia

**Target durasi:** 9:30–10:30
**Jumlah kata narasi:** ~1.480 (pada 150 kata/menit)
**Cast:** MONI (pemandu), ALVI (napas berhenti), CARDIO (tekanan turun)
**Otak:** direpresentasikan sebagai jaringan neuron abstrak yang bercahaya, bukan karakter
**Titik pivot:** dosis yang menghapus kesadaran juga menghapus napas
**Analogi skala:** kurang dari satu sendok makan cairan putih menghapus keberadaan Anda
**Etimologi:** anesthesia, amnesia, apnea, hypotension

---

## SCENE 1 — COLD OPEN
**0:00–0:22 | Bunuh miskonsepsi**

**[VISUAL]**
Layar gelap total, warna Abyss Teal. Garis EKG Amber masuk dari kiri, mengalir pelan, stabil.
Potong ke: ilustrasi orang tidur di kasur, glow cyan lembut, tenang.
Lalu ilustrasi orang di meja operasi, glow padam total, hanya garis EKG yang hidup.
Dua gambar bersanding. Yang kiri bernapas. Yang kanan tidak.

**[NARASI]**
People will tell you that general anesthesia is like falling asleep.

It is not.

When you sleep, your brain stays awake in its own way. It cycles. It dreams. It listens. Say your name loudly enough and you will surface.

Under general anesthesia, none of that happens. Your brain does not cycle. It does not dream. Someone could shout your name, cut through your abdominal wall, and spread your ribs apart, and there would be no one home to object.

For the next two hours, you will not exist.

Here is what actually happens in the ninety seconds it takes to get you there.

## SCENE 3 — HOLDING AREA
**0:28–1:35 | Latar manusia**

**[VISUAL]**
Ilustrasi ruang persiapan. Palet gelap, satu sumber cahaya cyan dari monitor kecil.
MONI berdiri di samping brankar, layar wajah menampilkan garis tenang.
Zoom pelan ke punggung tangan: kanula IV, glow cyan tipis mengalir masuk.

**[NARASI]**
You are lying on a trolley outside the operating room. You are cold, because operating rooms are always cold, and because you have not eaten since last night.

Someone has placed a small plastic tube into a vein on the back of your hand.

A machine beside you is watching three things: the electrical rhythm of your heart, the pressure inside your arteries, and the percentage of your red blood cells currently carrying oxygen. This machine will keep watching all three for the entire time you are gone.

The machine is beeping in time with your heart. Not roughly in time. Exactly in time. It is pitched to your pulse, which means every person in that room is currently listening to your heartbeat out loud.

Someone says, to nobody in particular, in the flat voice people use for things that must be on record: induction begins at seven forty two.

Then a hand closes around your wrist.

It is the same arm the tube is in. Drug going in on one side, fingers reading your pulse on the other, in the same limb, at the same moment. They are doing it by hand because the machine takes a few seconds to notice things that a fingertip already knows.

Someone asks you to count to twenty.

You will not reach thirty.

## SCENE 4 — ETIMOLOGI PERTAMA
**1:35–2:10**

**[VISUAL]**
Layar gelap. Kata `ANESTHESIA` muncul besar, Bone.
Kata membelah menjadi dua bagian dengan garis EKG sebagai pemisah:
`AN` (Cyan) dan `AISTHESIS` (Cyan).
Terjemahan muncul di bawah masing-masing.

**[NARASI]**
The word itself tells you what is being taken.

An, from Greek, meaning without. Aisthesis, meaning sensation.

Without sensation.

But that is only the first of three separate things that have to be switched off, and they are not the same thing at all. You need to stop feeling. You need to stop remembering. And you need to stop being aware.

A drug can do one of those without doing the others. That is why anesthesia is not one drug. It is a negotiation.

---

## SCENE 5 — OBAT MASUK
**2:10–3:00 | Eskalasi**

**[VISUAL]**
Cairan putih susu bergerak masuk lewat kanula, glow Bone terang di dalam pembuluh Deep Teal.
Ikuti alirannya: lengan, bahu, ke jantung.
CARDIO muncul, kondisi healthy, memompa cairan itu keluar ke seluruh tubuh.
Cairan menyebar ke atas, menuju siluet kepala.

**[NARASI]**
The syringe contains a white liquid. It looks like milk. Anesthetists have been calling it that for decades.

It enters the vein in your hand and travels up your arm, into the large veins of your chest, into the right side of your heart, through your lungs, back into the left side, and out into your aorta.

From there it takes one branch upward, into the arteries of your neck, and into your brain.

The entire journey takes about twenty seconds.

You are still counting. You have said four. You have said five.

## SCENE 6 — TURUN KE DUNIA MIKRO
**3:00–4:30 | Mekanisme**

**[VISUAL]**
Zoom masuk terus menerus, satu arah, tidak pernah keluar.
Kepala → jaringan neuron abstrak (titik-titik Cyan terhubung garis tipis, berkedip aktif, ramai).
Zoom lagi → satu celah sinaps, dua ujung neuron berhadapan.
Molekul propofol (bentuk kapsul Bone) menempel ke reseptor di membran.
Kanal terbuka. Partikel klorida Cyan mengalir masuk ke dalam sel.
Sel meredup.
Tarik keluar sedikit: seluruh jaringan neuron meredup bersamaan, garis penghubung putus satu per satu.

**[NARASI]**
Your brain contains roughly eighty six billion neurons, and at this moment they are all talking to each other.

That conversation is what you are.

Not any single neuron. Not any single region. Consciousness appears to be what happens when enough parts of the brain stay in contact with each other, exchanging information fast enough to build one unified picture of the world.

Break the contact, and the picture does not get blurry.

It stops.

The white liquid does not switch neurons off. It does something quieter than that. It finds a specific receptor sitting on the surface of your neurons, one that controls a gate. When the drug binds, that gate opens wider and longer than it should, and negatively charged chloride ions pour into the cell.

The inside of the neuron becomes more negative. And a neuron that is more negative is a neuron that is harder to fire.

One by one, the conversations get shorter. Then they stop answering each other. Then the network that was you falls silent.

You have said eleven. You have said twelve.

Then you say nothing.

And here is how the person beside you confirms that you are actually gone.

They do not shout your name. They do not shake your shoulder. They reach up and brush your eyelashes with a fingertip.

If the lashes flicker, some part of you is still in there. If nothing moves at all, you are not.

That is the test. The most delicate structure on your face, touched lightly enough that it would not wake a sleeping child, is what tells a doctor whether a human being is still present in the room.

## SCENE 7 — ARITMETIKA TERBUKA
**4:30–5:30 | Analogi skala**

**[VISUAL]**
Ilustrasi syringe di tengah layar. Angka muncul on-screen, Amber, Archivo Black.
Perbandingan visual: syringe kecil di samping siluet tubuh manusia penuh.
Lalu di samping sendok makan.

**[NARASI]**
Let us look at how much drug this actually takes.

For a healthy adult, a standard induction dose is around two milligrams for every kilogram you weigh. If you weigh seventy five kilograms, that is one hundred and fifty milligrams.

The solution is one percent, which means ten milligrams in every millilitre. One hundred and fifty milligrams comes out to fifteen millilitres.

Fifteen millilitres is one tablespoon.

Everything you have ever seen, every voice you recognise, every memory you own, and the continuous sense that you are someone at all, is suspended by slightly less than one tablespoon of white liquid.

And it holds for somewhere between five and ten minutes.

Which brings us to the problem.

---

## SCENE 8 — PIVOT
**5:30–7:00 | Titik pembalikan**

**[VISUAL]**
ALVI muncul, kondisi healthy, mengembang dan mengempis pelan, glow cyan stabil.
Ritme melambat. Melambat lagi. Berhenti. ALVI diam di posisi setengah kempis, glow meredup.
CARDIO berubah ke kondisi straining, lalu failing.
MONI berubah dari neutral ke alert ke alarm. Layar berubah amber lalu merah.
Garis EKG di bawah layar mulai kacau.

**[NARASI]**
The part of your brain that silences your thoughts is not a separate department from the part that runs your breathing.

The drug cannot tell them apart.

The same dose that removed your awareness reaches the cluster of cells in your brainstem that sets the rhythm of your breath, and it quiets those too. Your chest stops moving.

Apnea. A, without. Pnoia, breath.

At the same time, the walls of your blood vessels relax, and the pressure inside your arteries falls. Hypotension. Hypo, under. Tensio, pressure.

And the muscles at the back of your throat, the ones that hold your airway open every second of your life without you ever thinking about it, go slack. Your tongue falls backwards against the wall of your throat and closes it.

Now listen to the machine again.

It was never only counting your heartbeat. It was also singing your oxygen. The higher the note, the more oxygen your blood is carrying. And as the oxygen in your blood begins to fall, the note falls with it.

The pitch drops. Then it drops again.

Everyone in that room hears the note descending before anyone has looked at a single number on the screen. It is one of the most feared sounds in medicine, and it is not an alarm. It is just a tone, going down.

So here is what has actually happened. You are unconscious. You are not breathing. Your blood pressure is dropping. And your airway is blocked.

If nobody intervenes in the next few minutes, you die on that table.

The drug did not save you. The drug is the thing trying to kill you.

---

## SCENE 9 — RESOLUSI
**7:00–8:45**

**[VISUAL]**
Tangan (siluet Bone, tanpa wajah) memasang sungkup di wajah pasien.
Laringoskop masuk, glow cyan menerangi jalan napas.
Tabung endotrakeal masuk, terhubung ke ventilator.
ALVI kembali mengembang, kali ini didorong dari luar, ritmenya mekanis dan teratur.
Glow ALVI kembali. CARDIO kembali ke healthy.
MONI kembali ke neutral, garis EKG stabil lagi.

**[NARASI]**
Which is why there is a person standing at the head of the table whose entire job is the ninety seconds you just watched.

Before the drug was ever injected, they had already filled your lungs with pure oxygen, giving you a reserve to spend while you cannot breathe. Now they tilt your head back, lifting the tongue off the back of your throat, and hold a mask over your face to push oxygen in.

Then they take a curved metal blade with a light on the end of it. Laryngoscope. Larynx, the voice box. Skopein, to look.

They lift your tongue and jaw forward until they can see the opening of your windpipe, and they slide a tube through it. That tube goes to a machine that will now breathe for you, at a rate and a volume someone has chosen, for as long as the operation takes.

For the next two hours, every breath you take will be a decision made by another human being.

That is the actual job. Not putting you to sleep. Anyone can make you unconscious. Keeping you alive while you are is the difficult part.

---

## SCENE 10 — ZOOM OUT
**8:45–9:50**

**[VISUAL]**
Untuk pertama kalinya dalam video, kamera menarik keluar, bukan masuk.
Sinaps → jaringan neuron yang mulai menyala kembali, satu per satu → kepala → ruang operasi → siluet gedung rumah sakit.
Garis EKG Amber mengalir melintasi seluruh scene, stabil.

**[NARASI]**
When the operation ends, the drug is stopped. It drains out of your brain and into your fat and muscle. The gates close. The chloride stops flowing. The neurons become excitable again, and they start finding each other.

The network rebuilds itself. And you come back.

But here is the part that unsettles most people, including a lot of the people who do this for a living.

You will have no sense that any time passed at all. There is no darkness to remember, because darkness is still an experience and you did not have one. There will be the moment you said twelve, and then there will be a ceiling and a different room, and nothing in between.

Every day, in every hospital on earth, hundreds of thousands of people cross that line and come back. It is one of the most ordinary things medicine does.

And still.

The person standing at the head of that table has done this a thousand times. They know exactly which receptor the drug binds to. They know exactly how many millilitres are in the syringe, and how many seconds it will take to reach your brain, and what they will do in each of the four ways this can go wrong.

And in the moment before they push the plunger, a great many of them do something that appears in no textbook, no protocol, and no drug label.

They pray you come back.
